# S-006 — Knowledge-exchange study

**Version:** V4  
**Status:** synthetic research summary  
**Date:** 20 March 2026

The project team reviewed examples of recent cross-disciplinary work with participating employees. Participants most often attributed useful knowledge exchange to scheduled work reviews, access to visible project artefacts with sufficient context, and introductions by colleagues who understood the problem. Casual encounter helped people learn that other work existed, but rarely provided enough context to influence a project without a later structured discussion.

The study challenges the idea that encounter frequency alone explains useful exchange. It continues to support the broader intent of earlier cross-disciplinary learning.

## Method note

This is a small qualitative study intended to refine hypotheses. It is not sufficient to quantify organisation-wide behaviour.
